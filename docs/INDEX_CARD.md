# Index card

**Project:** Scriba Executive Desk, an AI executive secretary built on Scriba.

## Whose weekly job

The person who manages an executive's calendar: an executive assistant, a
chief of staff, or a founder doing it themselves. Every week they read
meeting requests arriving by email, check the executive's calendars, go back
and forth on times, book the meeting, and confirm it.

## Trigger

A new email arrives in the executive's Gmail or Outlook mailbox asking to
set up, move or cancel a meeting.

## Workflow

1. Read the incoming message and the rest of its thread.
2. Work out what is being asked: a new meeting, a reschedule or a
   cancellation, with whom, when, and for how long.
3. Check the executive's Google Calendar and Outlook Calendar for that time.
4. Detect conflicts across both calendars.
5. If the requested time is free, plan to book it. If not, pick alternative
   times inside the executive's working hours.
6. Show the executive the proposed reply and the proposed calendar change,
   and wait for approval. Nothing is sent or booked before approval.
7. On approval, create, update or cancel the event on the right calendar.
8. Reply in the original email thread, from the mailbox that received it.
9. Record what was done, and open a follow-up if an answer is still awaited.

## What successful completion means

- The requester receives one correct reply in the original thread: a
  confirmation, a set of alternative times, or an acknowledgement.
- The calendar shows exactly one correct event, or the correct change or
  cancellation, with no double booking on either calendar.
- No email was sent and no calendar entry was written without the
  executive's approval.
- The run is recorded with its steps, its outcome and its cost.
