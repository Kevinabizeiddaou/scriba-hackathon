# Index card

**Project:** Scriba Executive Desk, an autonomous executive secretary built
on Scriba.

**The one job:** turn an executive's incoming correspondence into verified
administrative outcomes.

## Whose weekly job

> **Job-holder: [to be supplied by the project lead: name, title, organisation]**

The role is the person who runs an executive's inbox and calendar: an
executive assistant, a chief of staff, or a founder doing it themselves.
Every day they read what arrived in the executive's mailboxes, decide what
each message needs, answer or draft answers, arrange and rearrange meetings,
note tasks and deadlines, chase replies that have not come, and tell the
executive what happened and what is waiting.

## Trigger

A new email arrives in the executive's Gmail or Outlook inbox.

Two secondary triggers start the same job on demand: the executive asks for
something in chat, and the daily briefing time is reached.

## Workflow

1. **Read** the incoming message and the rest of its thread.
2. **Triage** it: what kind of message it is (meeting request, needs a
   reply, task or deadline, information only, bulk) and how urgent.
3. **Decide the outcome** it needs: a reply, a meeting booked, moved or
   cancelled, a task or reminder, a follow-up to track, or nothing.
4. **Gather context:** the thread, the executive's Google and Outlook
   calendars, and existing tasks and commitments.
5. **Prepare the action:** draft the reply; for meetings, check availability
   on both calendars, detect conflicts and choose or propose times; write
   the task or reminder.
6. **Ask for approval.** Anything that sends an email or changes a calendar
   waits for the executive's approval, shown with exactly what will happen
   and why.
7. **Execute:** send the reply in the original thread from the mailbox that
   received it; create, update or cancel the event on the right calendar;
   create the task or reminder.
8. **Verify** that the provider accepted the action (the message was sent,
   the event exists as intended) and record the result.
9. **Follow up:** track replies that are awaited and raise them when they
   are overdue.
10. **Brief the executive** once a day: what arrived, what was done, what is
    waiting for approval, and what is overdue.

## What successful completion means

- Every incoming message ends in exactly one recorded outcome: replied,
  scheduled, turned into a task or reminder, tracked as a follow-up, or
  deliberately left with no action.
- Replies are correct, go to the right people, and sit in the original
  thread, sent from the mailbox that received the message.
- The calendar shows exactly the agreed event or change, with no double
  booking across Google and Outlook.
- No email was sent and no calendar entry was written without the
  executive's approval.
- Each action was verified against the provider and is in the audit history
  with its steps, outcome and cost.
- The daily briefing matches the audit history.
- Nobody had to repair anything afterwards. An approval is not a repair.
