# Customer voice-note questions (draft)

Questions to send to a real person who schedules meetings for a living: an executive assistant, a chief of staff, a founder who runs their own calendar. The aim is a one to two minute voice note in their own words.

## Ground rules

- Ask for consent to use the recording, and say where it will be played. Keep the consent message.
- Send the questions, not suggested answers. Do not script, edit for meaning, or re-record until the answer sounds better.
- Use only what a real person actually said. The twenty evaluation cases are synthetic fixtures and must never be presented as customer stories.
- If nobody has answered by the deadline, say so in the presentation. Do not substitute a made-up quote.

## Message to send

> Hi [name], we are building a tool that handles meeting requests for an executive: it reads the request, checks the calendar, drafts the reply, and asks for approval before sending anything. Could you record a one to two minute voice note answering the three questions below? We would like to play it in a short internal presentation this weekend. Fine to say no.

## The three to ask (pick these if they will only answer three)

1. Think of the last meeting that was painful to schedule. What happened, and how long did it take from the first email to a confirmed time?
2. What is the mistake you most fear when someone else, or something else, handles the calendar for you?
3. If a tool drafted the reply and the calendar change and waited for your one-click approval, what would it have to get right before you trusted it?

## Follow-ups, if they are willing to say more

About the work today:

4. Roughly how many meeting requests do you handle in a normal week? How many of them need more than one round of emails?
5. Which kinds take the most time: conflicts, time zones, rescheduling, vague requests, something else?
6. What do you check by hand every time, even when you are sure?

About trust and approval:

7. Which actions would you never let a tool take without asking you first? Which would you be happy for it to do alone?
8. When a tool asks for your approval, what do you need to see to decide in a few seconds?
9. Have you received an email that tried to trick you or an assistant into doing something? What did it look like?

After showing them the demo (only if they have seen it):

10. What in the demo matched how you actually work, and what did not?
11. Where would it break in your real inbox?
12. What would it be worth to you: hours saved, a monthly price, or not worth paying for?
